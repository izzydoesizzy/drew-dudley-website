## Desired Design

### Philosophy
- **Minimalist luxury brand approach** (like Mercedes — subtle, confident, don't oversell)
- **Functional over informational** — help people find what they need fast
- **No need to convince** — curated, intentional content only
- **Qualify before you meet** — don't waste time on sales calls with unqualified leads

### Color Palette
- Dark charcoal/black backgrounds (#0c0a08 + #111009 from Gathering Place)
- Yellow/gold accents (#c4922a / #d4a85a)
- Green accent
- Night-sky blue
- Nearly no rounded corners (2-4px, nearly square)

### Font
- **Current**: "Drew" in a font that coincidentally matches Drew Barrymore's show logo (he used it first, but people don't know that)
- **Desired**: West Wing font (same as The Gathering Place)
- Change across LinkedIn, Facebook, YouTube, Instagram for consistency

## Homepage Structure (Post-Redesign)

**Izzy recommended**: Keep speaking and Gathering Place separate (Gathering Place gets its own coming-soon page).

### Speaking Side (Main Focus)
**Four links. Period.**

1. **Wall Street Journal Bestselling Author** → Links to Amazon page (book sales not priority; bulk buys happen at events)
2. **Global Keynote Speaker** → Keynote detail page
3. **Bespoke Executive Development** → Gathering Place link
4. **TED Talk** → TED video (still drives fame)

### Keynote Page Detail
- **Live counter** (e.g., "1,052 keynotes delivered") — updates automatically when new speech added
- **Short statement** of testimonials (audience feedback snippets, not client case studies)
- **Sizzle reel** (pro video with statistics + engagement clips)
- **Looping testimonial videos** (audience reactions, not speakers talking about him)
- **Few paragraphs** of description (keep it minimal)
- **Interactive booking inquiry** → Not a form, a **wizard/quiz flow**

### Booking Wizard Features
- **Smart branching**: Different questions based on previous answers
- **Pre-qualification path**: Asks budget, organization size, past speaker spend, pro-bono status, etc.
- **Outcome**: Different results for different lead types:
  - High-quality, qualified lead → "Let's schedule a call. Here are 3 times." + email notification to Drew
  - Under-budget but legitimate → "Here are alternative options" (pro-bono discussion, reduced scope)
  - Not a fit → "Thanks, best of luck" (graceful decline)
- **Pricing transparency**: Shows estimated range upfront (e.g., "Based on your inputs: $12,500–$15,000") so people self-select
- **Optional video recording**: Instead of typing event description, let people record a 3-minute audio/video summary → Drew responds within 20 minutes with estimate

### Booking Calendar
- People see Drew's availability at a glance
- Days color-coded by price tier (low/mid/high season)
- "Virtual only" option for days when Drew is local but willing to do remote + stay home
- Not fully booked = you can click and inquire

## Design Notes

### What to Keep Minimal
- ❌ No "services" pages listing all workshop types
- ❌ No long descriptions of what each offering does (clients aren't reading)
- ❌ No "bios" or self-promotion copy
- ❌ No white space (Drew hates white space)
- ❌ No warped/distorted images

### What to Emphasize
- ✅ Clean, direct links (4 only)
- ✅ Social proof (testimonial clips from real audiences)
- ✅ Functional booking (calendar + wizard, not gatekeeping info)
- ✅ Transparency (show estimated pricing upfront)
